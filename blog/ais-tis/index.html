The part most people miss

Matching your AIS is necessary. It is not sufficient.

A clean reconciliation gets your numbers to agree with the department's. It does not, on its own, make the return correct. Here is where matching quietly falls short — and why judgement still matters.

◆ AIS shows
Share sales
₹42,00,000
✓ The return needs
Your actual gain
That figure is turnover, not profit. The taxable gain — after cost, holding period and grandfathering — may be a fraction of it, or a loss.
◆ AIS shows
Rent received
— nothing —
✓ The return needs
The full rent
If your tenant never deducted TDS, the AIS stays silent. The rent is still fully taxable and must be declared.
◆ AIS shows
Dividend, twice
reported by 2 sources
✓ The return needs
It counted once
Broker and company can each report the same dividend. Add both and you overstate income you'll then be taxed on.
◆ AIS shows
Foreign shares
not flagged
✓ The return needs
Schedule FA
RSUs or ESOPs in a foreign parent rarely surface as a foreign-asset alert. The disclosure is on you — and the penalty for missing it is separate from any tax.
◆ AIS shows
Property sale
full value, your PAN
✓ The return needs
Only your share
Sold jointly? The whole consideration can land against your name — but only your portion of the gain is yours to report.
◆ AIS shows
Interest, one line
net of TDS
✓ The return needs
Every source, classified
Five banks, small savings interest, reinvested FD interest — the correctly-headed figure rarely equals a single AIS entry.
